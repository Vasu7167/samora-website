The founder

Vasu Bansal

Founder and Builder-in-Chief

LinkedInEmail

Most sales careers are about optimising a playbook that already works. Mine has been the opposite. Starting from zero: no customers, no pipeline, no comparable, and no certainty about what the product even needs to be in that market.

Over the last decade I have worked on the ground in more than forty countries and done business in more than eighty markets, across the Middle East, South Asia, Africa, Europe and beyond. Every entry was different. Every market had its own way of deciding, its own trust threshold, its own definition of value. The job was to work that out fast, earn credibility from nothing, and turn ambiguity into revenue.

I led EMEA from the ground up, built partner ecosystems across emerging economies, ran P&L across regions, and sat across the table from decision makers in markets most companies would not know how to enter.

The product was never the bottleneck. Getting it into the right rooms, across cultures, without a map, always was.

And every quarter, the same two problems came back. Finding the right companies and the people who actually decide took weeks of manual work in every new country. And the pipeline we reported was built on what people typed into a CRM, not on what buyers actually did.

With AI reshaping every industry, the next wave of growth will come from companies going into markets they have never touched. Samora is the map for that. SAM finds the companies, reaches the buying group in their own language, and keeps a pipeline that only moves on evidence.

I built SamoraOS in stealth for ten months with early design partners, and we launched in October 2026. Alongside it, I advise early-stage startups on go-to-market, which keeps us close to the problem we solve.

What the work taught us

Five lessons, built into the product.

01

Every market has its own trust threshold

Who decides, how they like to be approached, what they need to see first. You learn it fast or you do not get in.

02

The product is rarely the bottleneck

Getting it into the right rooms is. That is a finding, reaching and writing problem, which software can solve.

03

GTM fails at the data first

Not strategy, not people. The numbers driving decisions were typed by someone avoiding a CRM update.

04

Reps are not the problem

They are trapped in systems that reward data entry over selling. Make the work the data and they get their day back.

05

Language is respect

A buyer in Bangkok or Munich answers someone who wrote to them properly, in their language.
